Do lab assays agree with what is known about disease variants?

Six multiplexed assays of variant effect checked against ClinVar

Amir Daneshpajouh, 2026-10-19. Research software; nothing here classifies a patient's variant.

The problem

Genetic tests find many variants that change a single amino acid in a protein.

For most of them nobody knows whether they cause disease. The report says variant of uncertain significance, and the patient and their doctor cannot act on it.

What a MAVE measures

A multiplexed assay of variant effect makes thousands of versions of a gene and measures, in cells, how well each version works.

The score measures function in a lab system. It is not a probability of disease.

How to check an assay

Take variants whose effect is already settled in ClinVar (pathogenic or benign, reviewed by at least two labs or an expert panel) and ask:

  • Does the assay score them apart? (ROC AUC: 0.5 is a coin toss, 1.0 is perfect.)
  • How much more often is an "abnormal" result seen for pathogenic variants? (OddsPath, which ClinGen maps to evidence levels: above 4.3 moderate, above 18.7 strong.)

Keeping the test honest

  • The analysis plan was committed before any score was compared with a label.
  • The cut-off between "abnormal" and "normal" for each variant was chosen on the other variants only (five-fold cross-fitting).
  • Some ClinVar labels may already rest on these assays. The test is repeated on ClinVar as it stood in January 2018, before any of the six assays was published.

What this does and does not show

  • Shows: agreement between assay scores and expert labels, with honest uncertainty.
  • Does not show: that an assay models the disease, which ClinGen asks experts to judge separately.
  • Next: more benign controls, for example from population frequencies, and labels whose evidence sources are recorded, so assay-informed labels can be removed directly.
